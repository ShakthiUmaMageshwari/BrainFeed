Logical Relationships

Deep dive explanation for Question 5

Concept Summary

This concept explores deductive reasoning with categorical propositions. Understanding how "all," "some," and "no" define relationships between sets is crucial for logical inference. It forms the basis of syllogisms used in high-level aptitude exams.

Key Points

  • 'All A are B' means A is a complete subset of B. Every member of A is inside B.
  • 'Some A are B' means there is an intersection; at least one common element exists.
  • 'No A are B' implies completely disjoint sets with 0% overlap.

Practical Example

Scenario: "If all cats are mammals, and some mammals are pets..."

The Explanation:

A connection between cats and pets is possible but not guaranteed. In logic, "Some" is the weakest link and doesn't force a relationship between the outer sets.

Why it's Right

The correct logic follows a direct Euler diagram approach where the intersection of two primary sets creates a mandatory third region. To be "Always True," the relationship must hold in every possible diagram configuration.
